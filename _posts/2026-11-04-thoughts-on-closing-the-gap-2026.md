The assistive technology industry has grown and evolved since 1982, but one thing is the same. Every single fall people come to the annual Closing The Gap conference in Minnesota to learn what is new in the field and to network with others. I was lucky enough to go last week. I would like to tell you my top five favorite things that I have seen.

Number five is the new PRC device line the Genesis. The icons that are on Minspeak have a new look to them and the on and off button is bigger so people with a physical disability can turn on and off by themselves. This tells me two things about PRC: they listen to AAC communicators, family members, and professionals and they aren't afraid to try new things. The Genesis has a space on the back of the device for the message window, so people can read the AAC Communicator's messages.

Number four hello AI. AI was everywhere and anywhere you looked this year. We see that AI is bad and the government should make rules and guidelines on TV, but at this conference people couldn't stop talking about the power of this and the positive side of it. AI can save you time and money by making things for you. I remember when I was growing up, my aides took hours of their own time to adjust my work so I could do it. Now it would have taken them minutes with the help of AI. I saw a new app in the exhibit hall built by "an uncle" called EchoBridge. The app was interesting and scary. The app has symbols on it. First I needed to answer questions about likes and my interests. Then the app remembers everything. The app gave me answers from my interests. I think that this is cool, but we need to remember that this app won't work for every environment and everyone. For example, I might not have this app for myself, because I talk to different kinds of people. They had some interesting workshops to do with AI and AAC too.

Number three is a diverse group of professionals. This year I noticed a lot more men at the conference than I have seen before (I have been going to CTG since 2016). Plus people from different backgrounds. I think this is just amazing to see because the people that we serve in the assistive technology community come from different backgrounds too, they should be able to see themselves into the people that they work with! It is very important to get an African American man who is a speech therapist, physical therapist, occupational therapist, aide, or teacher to a kid who is African American. I believe the trust from the family will be easier to get, because they will understand the values of the family better.

Number two: getting to talk to people in person. The assistive technology community is a small group of people, but we are all around the globe. It is great to connect with others that work in the same field. I chatted with my friends throughout the three days and I saw others who did the same thing.

Number one thankfulness to the family who put this amazing conference on each fall. This conference feels like a family reunion, because the people who run it make it this way.
